# Level 0 image-brief review

## Source sections used

The brief draws primarily from world-bible sections 5 (Space DNA categories), 7–10 (direct movement, continuity, scene data, and production pipeline), 11 (website, image, input, persistence, mobile, and frontier rules), and 13.1–13.3 (Level 0 DNA, unnamed continuous variation, and first-production scope). Sections 2, 8, 12, 15, and 17 were used to preserve fixed-world boundaries and distinguish settled from unresolved rules.

## Intentionally omitted

- Role assignments and general project governance
- Full fixed-world map theory beyond what follow-up continuity needs
- Save/reset/frontier implementation details that do not change raster production
- Discarded product directions already covered by the concise forbidden-drift list
- Long-term rare level-boundary design and production-frontier operations
- Change-log repetition

## Ambiguity and interpretation

- The world bible permits beauty and strong horror globally, while the milestone asks for a familiar, hollow, faintly unsettling opening. The brief preserves the wider emotional range but narrows only this first bundle.
- Level 0 can vary enormously in scale and function, but the first slice is intentionally conventional. Large or monumental variation was not treated as forbidden—only out of scope for this bundle.
- The source specifies a moderate lens but no fixed focal length. The production range of roughly 35–45 mm equivalent, with 40 mm used for metadata, is a provisional practical interpretation.
- “Late 1990s to mid-2000s consumer digital camera” describes character rather than a mandatory named camera or exact sensor simulation.
- Moist carpet is a recurring condition, not a requirement to show puddles or heavy damage.

## Potential over-constraint risks

The palette, four-element continuity checklist, and mostly empty object ecology could create repetitive scenes if applied mechanically. They should be treated as a level-wide grammar, not a prompt template that fixes every composition. The lens range and sparse-object preference are production defaults, not immutable canon rules.
