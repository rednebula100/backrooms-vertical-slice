# Level 0 image-production brief

## Identity

Level 0 — Threshold is an endless yellow interior assembled from the purposeless remains of inexpensive North American and Western office, retail-backroom, leased-commercial, meeting-facility, and common-corridor construction. Its base layer feels late 1980s–1990s, with occasional mismatched early-2000s maintenance. It resembles a facility made for people but cannot fully perform work, sales, waiting, storage, rest, or circulation.

## Materials and palette

Use yellowed wallpaper or cheap drywall in sickly yellow, pale cream, nicotine beige, or muted ochre; continuous brown/beige commercial carpet; off-white or yellowed acoustic ceiling tiles and metal grid; dark vinyl or low-cost wood base trim. Limited metal, plastic, and laminate details are allowed. Moisture, stains, patched surfaces, and mismatched repairs may appear without turning every scene into a ruin.

## Lighting

Fluorescent fixtures are the normal source: neutral white through sickly yellow-white, flat and spatially repetitive. Alignment may be slightly wrong but not random. Bright interiors are common; limited failed lights, distant brightness, or extended dimness are variations, not defaults. Normal exterior daylight is not available.

## Camera and medium

Final scene assets are landscape 4:3. Use a standing human viewpoint near 1.6–1.7 m and a moderate, non-fisheye lens around a 35–45 mm full-frame equivalent. The photographic baseline is a late-1990s to mid-2000s consumer digital camera: mild sensor noise, slight fluorescent white-balance error, limited dynamic range, and everyday material imperfection. Avoid cinematic grading, strong VHS/CCTV/glitch treatment, date overlays, and random changes of photographic medium.

## Spatial grammar

Short corridors, inexplicable turns, rooms of unequal width, half-open bays, partitions, and purposeless divided interiors are normal. Room and corridor identities may blur. Temporary symmetry should fail through small errors. Straight construction dominates, with occasional corners, curved walls, or round columns. Wrongness begins at the edge of plausibility: wall thickness, ceiling height, door placement, light lines, floor slope, distance, and proportion are slightly off. Extreme fantasy geometry is not ordinary Level 0.

## Object ecology and condition

Most scenes are empty. Common details include outlets, vents, switches, empty frames, molding, ceiling access panels, and traces of removed signs. Sparse desks, folding chairs, shelving, waste bins, dry water dispensers, unused counters, and blocked internal windows may appear occasionally. Strong landmarks are rare. Management is unexplained: some lights or patches look recent while adjacent surfaces have aged.

## Abnormality and emotion

Keep abnormality on three axes: missing purpose, near-plausible proportional error, and an architectural grammar that continues far longer than expected. Do not use probabilistic or observer-dependent spatial changes. The common range is familiarity, emptiness, boredom, weak unease, vague nostalgia, strange safety, and purposeless forward motion. Beauty, comfort, isolation, awe, and explicit horror remain possible but should not dominate this first slice.

## Movement-path grammar

Movement paths are real architecture: open frames, corridor continuations, turns beyond walls, stairs, low passages, partition gaps, or a clear direction across a larger space. They must read without pixel hunting or visible highlighting. Most scenes have one path; two are occasional; three or more are exceptional. Movement is normally direct and adjacent. The next image shows the camera immediately after passing the selected opening or turning with the route, facing forward, while preserving only the anchors needed to prove continuity.

## Allowed variation

Within Level 0, width, ceiling height, openness, partition density, object traces, cleanliness, moisture, scale, lighting regularity, wrongness, and emotional intensity can vary. Change only a few axes across a short scene bundle and keep the rest continuous. Do not pre-label every variation as a named zone.

## Forbidden drift

Do not introduce visible people or entities, corpses, gore, combat, chase staging, occult symbols, readable lore, brand logos, luxury interiors, futuristic systems, normal exterior scenery, extreme ruin, large debris fields, or another famous Backrooms level's signature. Ordinary scenes should retain at least three of: yellow wall treatment, brown/beige carpet, acoustic tile ceiling, fluorescent lighting. Do not turn the space into a hotel, school, hospital, residence, or heavy industrial facility.

## First scene

Begin in a recognizable but original yellow Level 0 interior. Present two natural, irreversible movement paths with different form, depth, visibility, and screen weight. Architecture alone must make both routes understandable. Neither may appear correct, safe, evil, primary, symmetrical, highlighted, or staged as a pair of menu choices. Keep the room mostly empty and the wrongness weak.

## Immediate follow-up continuity

For each starting path, move the camera only a few meters to the moment immediately after traversal. Preserve camera height, lens character, photographic medium, relevant wall or partition edges, opening dimensions, carpet direction, ceiling-grid direction, fluorescent alignment, trim, and movement direction. Allow small spatial evolution only. Do not show the discarded route, the area behind the player, a landmark, a different facility category, a new level, or a dramatic lighting/style shift.
